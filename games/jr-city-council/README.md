# Junior City Council 🏛️

**Khaemenes Elementary · Grades 1–5 · Civics**

Junior City Council is a local-first civics learning app in which children:

**hear a neighborhood problem → listen to several viewpoints → compare proposed solutions → cast a mock vote → explain their reasoning → design a public-service poster**

## One Nation For All inspiration

This app was built after inspecting the One Nation For All `apps` collection and, specifically, the existing `Elementary_Kids_Vote_index.html`.

The elementary voting app already includes:
- Voti the Owl
- mock voting
- child-friendly issues
- candidates with issue positions
- issue selection
- ballots
- a voting result flow

Its issue examples include clean water, parks/playgrounds, schools, animals, and recycling.

Junior City Council keeps that child-friendly civics spirit while changing the learning goal from **choosing a candidate** to **deliberating about a local public problem**.

## Core learning cycle

1. Present a neighborhood problem.
2. Hear multiple child-friendly stakeholder viewpoints.
3. Compare two proposed solutions.
4. Cast a mock city-council vote.
5. Explain the reason for the choice.
6. Identify which viewpoints were considered.
7. Design a public-service poster.
8. Save evidence to a local civic portfolio.

## Grade progression

### Grade 1
Community helpers, simple public problems, common rules, and the idea that people can have different needs.

### Grade 2
Listen to multiple viewpoints, compare two solutions, vote, and explain one reason.

### Grade 3
Compare benefits and tradeoffs and distinguish public needs from preferences.

### Grade 4
Analyze fairness, responsibilities, limited resources, and short- versus long-term effects.

### Grade 5
Use evidence-based civic reasoning, stakeholder analysis, tradeoffs, constraints, and stronger public-policy explanations.

## Neighborhood topics

Built-in case families include:
- Parks & playgrounds
- Clean water
- School supplies
- Animal care
- Recycling
- Safer streets

Each case includes multiple viewpoints and two neutral, child-appropriate proposals.

## Neutral civics design

The app is intentionally nonpartisan.

It does not:
- promote a real candidate
- promote a political party
- collect real voter data
- register children to vote
- simulate actual election eligibility
- direct children toward a real-world political position

The goal is civic reasoning, listening, public problem solving, and understanding voting as one decision-making tool.

## Public-service poster studio

Learners can create a service poster with:
- title
- message
- call to action
- color theme
- topic icon

The poster can be printed with the rest of the council session.
