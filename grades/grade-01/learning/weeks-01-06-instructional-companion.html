Week 01 · Welcome to First Grade Scholars

Essential question: How do first graders learn with courage, kindness, and careful work?

Literacy: names, classroom print, listening, speaking, sentence frames. Mathematics: count, compare, sort, and explain strategies. Inquiry: classroom community, routines, maps, and responsibilities.

Project: My First Grade Scholar Shield.

  1. Monday · Follow a routine, listen for meaning, speak in a complete sentence, and count a set of objects.

    Model: Model a routine for starting a lesson. Say each step aloud, then count 5 pencils by moving one pencil into a row for each number word.

    Try independently: Student chooses one routine, draws the steps in order, and writes or dictates one sentence: “A good scholar ____.” Then count a new set of 7–10 safe classroom objects.

    Evidence: A four-step routine drawing plus one complete sentence and a counted set.

  2. Tuesday · Count two sets, compare which has more or fewer, and explain the comparison.

    Model: Match 5 counters to 7 counters. Say: “Both groups were counted. The group of 7 has more because 2 counters are left without a match.”

    Try independently: Create two sets between 1 and 10 using household or classroom objects. Count them, circle the set with more, and complete: “___ has more than ___ because ___.”

    Evidence: Two student-created comparison sets with a labeled drawing or number sentence.

  3. Wednesday · Use labels and positional language to describe familiar places and objects.

    Model: Draw a rectangle for a table, a circle for a pencil cup, and a square for a book. Label each item and say where one item is compared with another.

    Try independently: Create a simple map with at least four labeled places or objects. Use one position word in an oral or written sentence.

    Evidence: A labeled mini-map of the student's learning space.

  4. Thursday · Create a personal learning artifact and explain choices with evidence from the week's work.

    Model: Show a sample shield and say: “I drew a book because I am practicing reading. My evidence is the sentence I wrote on Monday.”

    Try independently: Finish the shield. Add labels or dictated sentences. Rehearse a 30-second explanation of two shield sections.

    Evidence: Completed First Grade Scholar Shield with labels and/or dictated explanations.

  5. Friday · Demonstrate the week's skills independently and reflect on evidence of learning.

    Model: Model one fresh comparison problem and one fresh routine explanation. Think aloud briefly, then stop and let the student complete the next example independently.

    Try independently: Student completes a short performance check: explain a routine, count a fresh set, compare two groups, and interpret a simple map.

    Evidence: Friday mastery evidence: performance responses plus the completed Scholar Shield.

Accessible learning: Learners may speak, point, build with counters or letter cards, draw, type, write, or dictate their answers. Adults model first, then invite independent explanation and revision.

Assessment boundary: These companion tasks are optional formative support; only the existing official Academy quiz records weekly mastery.

Week 02 · Letters, Sounds, and Counting to 20

Essential question: How do letters, sounds, and numbers help us explain what we notice?

Literacy: letter names, consonant sounds, short vowel listening, sight words. Mathematics: count to 20, one more, one less, number order. Inquiry: noticing patterns in classroom tools and books.

Project: Alphabet and Number Pattern Book.

  1. Monday · Match letters to beginning sounds

    Model: Model how m-a-t blends to mat; distinguish the name M from the sound /m/.

    Try independently: Read sat and map from letter tiles; draw an object beginning with s.

    Evidence: Illustrated consonant sound matching card

  2. Tuesday · Count to twenty, find one more or less

    Model: Build 14 with a ten-frame and four counters; add one to make 15 and remove one to make 13.

    Try independently: Represent 12, 17 and 19 with ten-frames and label one more and one less.

    Evidence: Three counted sets with neighbor-number labels

  3. Wednesday · Identify a repeating pattern unit

    Model: Place blue-red-blue-red blocks; circle the blue-red unit and predict the next block.

    Try independently: Invent a new ABC pattern and deliberately add one error to explain and correct.

    Evidence: Illustrated pattern with repeating unit outlined

  4. Thursday · Produce an Alphabet and Number Pattern Book

    Model: Demonstrate a four-page book: S for sun, 16 as ten plus six, a 13–15 number line and an AB shape pattern.

    Try independently: Make a four-page labeled book with a new consonant, teen number, number order and repeating shape rule.

    Evidence: Labeled Alphabet and Number Pattern Book

  5. Friday · Independently demonstrate Week 2 skills

    Model: Blend the unfamiliar CVC word pen; build 18 as ten plus eight and extend a dot-star pattern.

    Try independently: Read two unseen CVC words, count a new collection within 20, and explain a repeating rule.

    Evidence: Independent sound, number and pattern evidence

Accessible learning: Learners may speak, point, build with counters or letter cards, draw, type, write, or dictate their answers. Adults model first, then invite independent explanation and revision.

Week 03 · Short Vowels and Addition Stories

Essential question: How can sounds and number stories help us read and solve?

Literacy: short a and e words, blending, beginning/middle/end. Mathematics: addition within 10 using objects and drawings. Inquiry: family stories and classroom examples.

Project: Short Vowel Story Mat.

  1. Monday · Blend short a and e in CVC words

    Model: Point to p-a-n, blend pan, swap the middle tile for e and read pen; identify the changed sound.

    Try independently: Sort sat, set, man and men by their middle vowel; independently read a fresh word.

    Evidence: Vowel comparison word board

  2. Tuesday · Solve joining stories within ten

    Model: Join three red counters and four blue counters, draw both groups and record 3+4=7.

    Try independently: Solve a new 4+3 word story with objects, picture and true equation.

    Evidence: Illustrated joining story with labeled addends

  3. Wednesday · Retell a story in three meaningful events

    Model: Model first a pencil was lost, next classmates searched, finally it was found by a table; pair details with pictures.

    Try independently: Listen to another short school or family story and retell first/next/last, identifying text evidence.

    Evidence: Three sequential picture cards and captions

  4. Thursday · Create a Short Vowel Story Mat

    Model: Model a story about Sam with two caps and three more, pictured as 2+3=5; read the short a in Sam.

    Try independently: Write or dictate two brief story sentences, draw joining groups and show a total at most 10.

    Evidence: Original Short Vowel Story Mat and equation

  5. Friday · Transfer short-vowel decoding and addition

    Model: Blend p-e-t into pet and verify 6+2=8 with eight counters, then write the equation.

    Try independently: Read two unseen short-a/e words, solve a new joining story and check the answer.

    Evidence: New phonics and addition work sample

Accessible learning: Learners may speak, point, build with counters or letter cards, draw, type, write, or dictate their answers. Adults model first, then invite independent explanation and revision.

Assessment boundary: These companion tasks are optional formative support; only the existing official Academy quiz records weekly mastery.

Week 04 · More Short Vowels and Subtraction Stories

Essential question: How do readers and mathematicians notice what changes?

Literacy: short i, o, and u words, CVC decoding, rereading. Mathematics: subtraction within 10, take apart, compare. Inquiry: observing changes in simple situations.

Project: Change Story Flipbook.

  1. Monday · Decode short i, o and u words

    Model: Blend p-i-n into pin, h-o-t into hot and c-u-p into cup; identify the middle vowel sounds.

    Try independently: Build sit, hop, sun and cup with movable letters and match them to simple illustrations.

    Evidence: CVC sound-change strip

  2. Tuesday · Model subtraction as taking away

    Model: Start with eight counters, remove three, draw the remainder and record 8−3=5.

    Try independently: Solve 9−4 and 7−2 with counters and verify each by addition.

    Evidence: Original take-away story with a checked equation

  3. Wednesday · Compare before and after observations

    Model: Compare drawings of seven books and five books; state two fewer without assuming who moved them.

    Try independently: Make a before/after record showing observed counts, a subtraction equation and a cautious explanation.

    Evidence: Labeled observation and change diagram

  4. Thursday · Publish a Change Story Flipbook

    Model: Model a three-page story: six dots; two crossed out; four remain; write 6−2=4.

    Try independently: Draw, write or dictate a three-page story with a short-i/o/u word and subtraction within ten.

    Evidence: Complete Change Story Flipbook

  5. Friday · Independently verify decoding and subtraction

    Model: Read lid and sun; remove six from nine counters and add three back to check.

    Try independently: Read unseen CVC words, solve a new take-away problem and explain how you verified it.

    Evidence: Independent decoding and corrected subtraction work

Accessible learning: Learners may speak, point, build with counters or letter cards, draw, type, write, or dictate their answers. Adults model first, then invite independent explanation and revision.

Assessment boundary: These companion tasks are optional formative support; only the existing official Academy quiz records weekly mastery.

Week 05 · Families, Communities, and Teen Numbers

Essential question: How do people and numbers work together in groups?

Literacy: characters, setting, retell, questions about text. Mathematics: teen numbers as ten and some more. Inquiry: families, helpers, rules, and community roles.

Project: Community Helper Number Mural.

  1. Monday · Name characters, setting and community helper roles

    Model: Read an inclusive library story; identify the visitor, librarian, place and helpful action.

    Try independently: Retell two important events in a different community story, citing an actual story detail.

    Evidence: Character, setting and helper evidence card

  2. Tuesday · Represent teen numbers as a ten and extra ones

    Model: Show 16 with a full ten-frame and six loose counters, then write 10+6=16.

    Try independently: Model 13, 15 and 18, label each full ten and the extra ones.

    Evidence: Accurate teen number drawing and labels

  3. Wednesday · Explain a community responsibility

    Model: Compare shared supplies before and after visitors put them away; identify how that action helps.

    Try independently: Choose a library, park or neighborhood role and explain one realistic helpful action.

    Evidence: Respectful role-and-action explanation

  4. Thursday · Design a Community Helper Number Mural

    Model: Illustrate a park volunteer with twelve seed packets: ten together and two separate; label 10+2=12.

    Try independently: Draw another helper and a number from 11–19; show the number as 10 plus extra ones.

    Evidence: Community Helper Number Mural with 10+n model

  5. Friday · Retell a story and independently model teen numbers

    Model: Read a new community story; identify who, where and what changed; model 17 as 10+7.

    Try independently: Retell three ordered events and represent a different teen number with a ten-frame.

    Evidence: Portfolio retelling and ten-and-ones demonstration

Accessible learning: Learners may speak, point, build with counters or letter cards, draw, type, write, or dictate their answers. Adults model first, then invite independent explanation and revision.

Week 06 · Weather, Seasons, and Data

Essential question: How can we use words, numbers, and pictures to describe weather?

Literacy: informational text features, weather words, oral report. Mathematics: collect data, picture graphs, compare more/less. Inquiry: weather observations and seasonal patterns.

Project: Class Weather Graph.

  1. Monday · Describe weather with observed evidence

    Model: Point to a sky picture: 'I see dark clouds' is observation while 'It may rain' is prediction.

    Try independently: Record one safe, dated sky observation and distinguish it from a forecast.

    Evidence: Dated observation with sky symbol and caption

  2. Tuesday · Collect weather data using tallies

    Model: Count five fictional days: sunny, sunny, cloudy, rainy, sunny; tally III, I and I.

    Try independently: Sort a new set of five illustrated days, tally by type and compare category totals.

    Evidence: Labeled weather tally chart

  3. Wednesday · Compare weather and seasonal patterns

    Model: Show two winter days, one sunny and one rainy; explain that one day cannot determine the whole season.

    Try independently: Compare two short weather logs and state one difference and one limitation.

    Evidence: Two dated weather comparisons

  4. Thursday · Create a picture graph with a one-day key

    Model: Graph three sunny, one cloudy and one rainy symbol; label that each picture represents one day.

    Try independently: Turn a new six-day weather tally into a graph with title, labels and a key.

    Evidence: Class Weather Graph with readable legend

  5. Friday · Interpret fresh weather data and make safe decisions

    Model: Read a graph where two sunny and two windy days tie; note that the sample does not predict next month.

    Try independently: Independently describe the difference between categories and identify an adult-supported safety action for severe weather.

    Evidence: Weather graph interpretation and safety explanation

Accessible learning: Learners may speak, point, build with counters or letter cards, draw, type, write, or dictate their answers. Adults model first, then invite independent explanation and revision.

Assessment boundary: These companion tasks are optional formative support; only the existing official Academy quiz records weekly mastery.
