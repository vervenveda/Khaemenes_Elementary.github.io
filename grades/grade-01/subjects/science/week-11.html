Week 11: Moon observations and sky patterns

Curiosity · Observation · Evidence · Safe investigation

Course overview← Previous weekNext week →
Essential question

What can repeated Moon observations tell us?

Learning target: I can ask a clear question, use observations or supplied data as evidence, and explain what my evidence suggests.

Prior learning: Connect to something seen at home, outdoors, in a book, or in an earlier week. A learner may answer through drawing, speech, movement, objects, or writing.

Five-day lesson plan

Monday · Wonder

Notice and predict

Look at supplied Moon photographs and notice its visible shape and position.

Record: Draw or say what you notice, then make one prediction before testing.

Tuesday · Learn

Build a model

Discuss how a series of observations is more useful than one picture.

Practice: Teach the idea back using a labeled drawing, objects, or a short oral explanation.

Wednesday · Investigate

Gather evidence

Arrange dated Moon images in chronological order without claiming a full explanation from a single image.

Data: Record a count, comparison, observation, drawing, or supplied-data result. Say how it was gathered.

Thursday · Design and revise

Try an improvement

Create a Moon observation calendar from a provided image set.

Revise: Change one feature or revise one idea, and explain why.

Friday · Explain

Share the evidence

Ordered image sequence and a sentence naming a visible pattern.

Sentence frame: “I think ___ because I observed ___.” Add a label, number, or detail that makes the evidence clear.

Access and differentiation

Choose a way to show learning

Foundation: use picture choices, a modeled example, and adult scribing. Core: complete the observation and evidence record independently with ordinary support. Extension: compare a second example, identify a limitation, or propose another test.

Science evidence notebook

Save one artifact that shows your own thinking. A grown-up may write the learner's spoken words without replacing them with the adult's answer.

Safety note: Use safe daylight or adult-supervised evening observation. If materials or adult support are not available, use photographs, drawings, or supplied data instead.
