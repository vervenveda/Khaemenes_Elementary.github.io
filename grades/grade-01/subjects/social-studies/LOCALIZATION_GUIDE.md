# Grade 1 Social Studies · State & Local Localization Guide

## Academy rule

Grade 1 is nationally portable and **state-localizable by requirement**. The national disciplinary core remains canonical. Educators localize appropriate examples, source sets, maps, public institutions, historical cases, civic symbols, and community evidence to the learner's state/local context.

Localization may enrich or substitute examples; it may **not** remove national core competencies, reduce lesson depth, or lower the Academy's 80% mastery threshold.

## Required localization checkpoints

- **Weeks 3–4 · Roles and community cooperation:** use real or teacher-curated local public/community roles where appropriate.
- **Weeks 11–12 · Community, state, nation, world / characteristics of place:** identify the learner's state on maps; add state/local physical and human features.
- **Weeks 15–16 · Family/community history and change:** use public local-history photographs, maps, museums, libraries, archives, or fictional alternatives. Never require private family history.
- **Weeks 26–29 · Civic memory, documents, government, elections:** include state/local symbols or landmarks, age-appropriate state government examples, and a local/state election-process example while retaining U.S. national civics.
- **Weeks 30–31 · Environments and community change:** use a state/local environmental or planning example.
- **Weeks 34–35 · Service and evidence-to-action:** let the civic example address a safe, nonpartisan local/community issue.

## What must stay national

Students still need the same Grade 1 inquiry, civics, geography, history, economics, culture, information-literacy, and informed-action competencies regardless of state. The state overlay adds relevance; it does not replace the national core.

## Indigenous and local history safeguard

When local/state examples touch Indigenous history, use the specific nation/community name when known and include present-day continuity. Prefer tribal nation or well-contextualized museum/archive sources over generic cultural summaries.

## Privacy

Do not require home addresses, immigration/citizenship status, religion, political affiliation, family finances, or private family records. Public, fictional, or teacher-curated examples are valid.
