# Grade 1 Social Studies · National Portability Crosswalk

The course is designed to meet a broad national Grade 1 Social Studies envelope rather than mirror one state's sequence.

## NCSS C3
- Questions and inquiry appear every week.
- Disciplinary concepts explicitly include civics, economics, geography, and history.
- Source/evidence work occurs every Wednesday and within research/capstone weeks.
- Conclusions and informed action appear through civic decisions, community problem solving, service, and capstone work.

Official C3: https://www.socialstudies.org/standards/c3

## New York Grade 1
The course covers family and other families now and long ago, cultural diversity, responsible citizenship, authority/rules/laws, maps/directions, family history and sources, and early economic decision-making.
Reference: https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/social-studies

## California Grade 1
The course covers citizenship rights/responsibilities, direct and representative rule-making, fair play and respect, map/globe/location skills, cardinal directions and map symbols, physical/human characteristics of place, civic symbols/landmarks/documents, and historical continuity.
Reference: https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/hs/

## Florida and broader portability
Civics, geography, history, economics, culture, primary-source awareness, public symbols, jobs, money, needs/wants, rules/laws, authority, and responsible decision-making remain embedded throughout.
Reference: https://www.fldoe.org/academics/standards/subject-areas/social-studies/

## Evidence and assessment
Weekly checks are open-book and require 80% mastery. The midterm and final are closed-book, randomized, teacher-supervised assessments. Reading accommodations may be provided when they do not disclose answers; Social Studies knowledge should not be confused with reading disability or developing fluency.

## State/local localization

State localization is required across the Khaemenes Social Studies sequence. Each grade keeps a broad national disciplinary core while adapting appropriate examples, sources, maps, institutions, Indigenous histories, biographies, civic cases, and community evidence to the learner's state/locality. Localization does not lower the 80% mastery threshold or delete national competencies.
