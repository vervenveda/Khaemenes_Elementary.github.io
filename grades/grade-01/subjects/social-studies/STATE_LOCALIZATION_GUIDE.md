# Grade 1 Social Studies · State & Local Localization Guide

State/local localization is a **required design feature** of the Khaemenes Academy Social Studies program. The national Grade 1 disciplinary core remains canonical; educators adapt place-based examples, maps, public institutions, biographies, symbols, community history, and civic evidence to the learner's state and locality.

## Priority localization points
- **Weeks 3–4:** school/community roles, local services, cooperation, and public/community institutions.
- **Weeks 9–12:** maps, cardinal directions, community/state/nation/world nesting, physical and human characteristics of the learner's state and region.
- **Weeks 15–16:** local/community history through public photographs, objects, maps, oral-history collections, libraries, museums, and archives.
- **Weeks 26–29:** state/local symbols and landmarks; local, state, and national government; voting/elections/representation using nonpartisan age-appropriate examples.
- **Weeks 30–31:** state/local environments, community change, planning, hazards, conservation, and public spaces.
- **Weeks 34–35:** service and informed-action projects may address a safe teacher-approved local need.

## What may be localized
Educators may substitute or add:
- state flag/seal, capital, landmarks, public buildings, parks, waterways, regions, and maps;
- current state/local public services and institutions;
- state/local historical photographs, artifacts, oral histories, and biographies;
- specific Indigenous nation(s) and homelands connected to the region, using current nation/tribal sources where available;
- local environmental and community-planning examples;
- state/local civic symbols and commemorations studied with historical context.

## Florida example
Florida implementations may use local maps and coasts/wetlands, the Florida state capital and symbols, municipal/county/state services, Florida public lands and waterways, age-appropriate local-history sources, and specific Florida Indigenous histories/nations where relevant.

## Safeguards
Do not require a home address, immigration/citizenship status, religion, political affiliation, family finances, or private family history. Use fictional/public examples when needed. Keep election/civic learning nonpartisan. Do not portray Indigenous peoples only in the past.

## Non-negotiables
Localization does not remove national core objectives, lower the 80% mastery threshold, or replace evidence-based inquiry with local trivia.
