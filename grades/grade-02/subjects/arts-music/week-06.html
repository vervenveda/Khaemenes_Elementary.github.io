Evidence Studio · Week 06

Study: Scientific illustrators draw what they observe, not what they imagine. Look at a real leaf or a simple leaf photograph. Trace its outer contour, central vein and smaller branching veins; label each part with a straight pointer line. In music, a four-beat pattern can be notated as clap, rest, clap, tap: each symbol represents one beat.

Model: Draw one leaf twice. The first drawing omits veins; the revised drawing adds three observable details and labels. Under it, write four beat boxes: clap | rest | clap | tap. Perform the sequence slowly and check that there are four beats.

Independent practice: Make your own observed-object drawing with at least three accurate labels. Compose, mark, and perform a four-beat rhythm using sounds or silent movements. Explain how the visual labels and beat symbols help someone else repeat your work.

Reflect: Name an observation you corrected after looking again; show the revised mark. Keep the drawing and rhythm as optional evidence. Existing graded assessments remain unchanged.

Monday: Visual Art: scientific drawing and labels

Objective: Students build visual art skill through scientific drawing and labels within the weekly theme Informational Text and Measurement.

Activity: Model, practice, discuss, and create evidence for scientific drawing and labels.

Teacher script: Introduce Visual Art in student-friendly language. Model one clear example and think aloud. Students practice with talk, tools, writing, drawing, movement, or design. Close with one evidence sentence or demonstration.

Evidence: Work sample or oral demonstration showing visual art progress.

Depth Lab · Day 1: Observe a real or pictured leaf and name contour, central vein, and branching veins.

Reasoning check: Show the evidence behind your decision, identify a likely error and revise your work. This is practice; established scores and mastery remain separate.
