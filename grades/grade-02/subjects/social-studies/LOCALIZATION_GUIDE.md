# Grade 2 Social Studies · State & Local Localization Guide

## Academy rule

Grade 2 is nationally portable and **state-localizable by requirement**. Preserve the canonical national course and the 80% mastery gates; localize examples, maps, sources, public institutions, historical cases, Indigenous nations, biographies, and capstone evidence where appropriate.

## Required localization checkpoints

- **Week 4 · Community change:** use a public local/state then-and-now map, photograph, landmark, or record when available.
- **Weeks 5–8 · Geography:** place the learner's state within North America; add state/local map elements, landforms, waterways, urban/suburban/rural examples, land use, and human-environment interaction.
- **Weeks 9–12 · Indigenous nations:** identify specific nation(s), homelands, and present-day communities connected to the state/region where historically appropriate. Use nation-created or well-contextualized sources where available.
- **Weeks 13–16 · Migration, colonization, immigration:** add one or more state/local cases while retaining the broader North American comparison.
- **Weeks 19–22 · Government and citizenship:** add state/local government institutions, services, symbols, documents, or civic processes while retaining U.S. constitutional foundations.
- **Weeks 27–30 · People who make a difference:** include state/local public servants, African American contributors, community leaders, institutions, or civic problem-solving cases.
- **Week 35 · Research/community-action capstone:** permit a state/local question or community issue when safe, nonpartisan, and evidence-ready.

## Non-negotiable national core

Localization must not remove the Grade 2 inquiry, North American geography, Indigenous-history, migration, civics, economics, culture, information-literacy, or informed-action outcomes. Local examples must be assessed with the same 80% mastery expectations.

## Privacy and inclusion

Never require immigration/citizenship status, street address, private family history, religion, political affiliation, or family finances. Use public, fictional, or teacher-curated evidence when needed.
