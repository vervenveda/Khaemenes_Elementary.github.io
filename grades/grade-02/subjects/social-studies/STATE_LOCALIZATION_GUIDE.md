# Grade 2 Social Studies · State & Local Localization Guide

State/local localization is a **required design feature**. Grade 2 keeps its national core in community inquiry, North American geography, Indigenous histories, migration, government, economics, culture, information literacy, and civic action while using the learner's state/locality for meaningful evidence and case studies.

## Priority localization points
- **Weeks 1–4:** community questions, then-and-now maps/photos, local change, public places, and community evidence.
- **Weeks 5–8:** use state/regional maps for grids, physical/political/thematic map practice, land use, transportation, urban/suburban/rural patterns, and human-environment interaction.
- **Weeks 9–12:** study specific Indigenous nation(s)/homelands connected to the state/region, including present-day continuity and nation-created sources where available.
- **Weeks 13–16:** add state/local migration, settlement, immigration, labor, and cultural-contribution stories while retaining broader North American context.
- **Weeks 19–22:** compare local/state/federal public institutions, state/local laws/services, civic symbols, public service, and constitutional connections.
- **Weeks 27–30:** include state/local public servants, African American contributors, institutions, biographies, and community problem-solving cases.
- **Weeks 31–35:** use local oral histories, communication change, media examples, cultural comparison, and a safe local research/action capstone.

## What may be localized
Educators may substitute or add current maps, state/local government examples, public institutions, historical sources, biographies, tribal-nation sources, migration histories, economic examples, transportation networks, cultural institutions, and environmental/community issues.

## Florida example
Florida implementations may use Florida physical/political/thematic maps; coasts, wetlands, rivers, cities and rural areas; specific Indigenous histories and contemporary tribal nations; local/state migration and immigration stories; Florida public institutions and symbols; African American Florida contributors; ports, tourism, agriculture, and transportation as economic examples.

## Safeguards
Never require disclosure of immigration/citizenship status, street address, family finances, religion, political affiliation, or private family history. Do not treat one cultural or migration story as universal. Keep civic/election examples nonpartisan.

## Non-negotiables
State localization enriches examples and evidence; it does not remove North American/national competencies, erase Indigenous sovereignty/continuity, or weaken the 80% mastery system.
