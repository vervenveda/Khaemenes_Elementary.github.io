Week 06 · Integrated Projects

Division and Fair Sharing

How do we divide fairly and explain our process?

Evidence Studio · Week 06

Essential question: Does dividing equally always mean making the same number of groups?

Study and vocabulary: A dividend is the total, a divisor describes the number of equal groups or the size of each group, and the quotient is the result. Fair participation also means a person can access the supplies or role they need; identical tasks are not always accessible.

Worked model: A fictional class has 24 drawing pencils and 6 tables. Place one pencil at each table repeatedly until all are shared: each receives 4. Record 24 ÷ 6 = 4 and verify 6 × 4 = 24. Draw six circles with four marks in each. Contrast 24 pencils placed in groups of 6: there are 4 groups. The numbers match, but the question and interpretation change.

Guided reasoning: A classmate says six tables receive six pencils each. Check: 6 × 6 = 36, which exceeds the 24 available. Show how the multiplication check catches the error. Explain what would change if there were eight tables instead.

Independent project: Plan a supply station for 30 notebooks and 5 teams. Produce an equal-group diagram, a division sentence and a multiplication check; then redesign the layout for 6 teams. Include large-print labels or another access feature without requiring students to disclose personal needs. Compare the two plans in complete sentences.

Challenge: You have 26 notebooks and 6 teams. Can every notebook be shared equally as a whole item? Show 26 = 6 × 4 + 2, propose a responsible plan for two extras, and defend why you did not silently give some teams more.

Presentation and evidence: Give a brief explanation with a labeled model, both checks, a fairness statement, and one corrected misconception. Peers can respond using “I see...” and “Have you considered...?” Evidence may be written, drawn or spoken; formal mastery remains in the established assessment system.
