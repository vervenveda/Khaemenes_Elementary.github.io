# Grade 3 Social Studies · State & Local Localization Guide

## Academy rule

Grade 3 is nationally portable and **state-localizable by requirement**. The national disciplinary sequence remains canonical. Educators localize appropriate state/community history, geography, Indigenous nations, public institutions, biographies, data, civic cases, and capstone evidence.

Localization may enrich or substitute examples and sources. It may not delete core competencies, eliminate the comparative world-community strand, or reduce the Academy's 80% mastery expectations.

## Required localization checkpoints

- **Week 4 · Local history/community change:** investigate a public local/state history question using maps, photographs, archives, museums, newspapers, oral-history collections, or public records.
- **Weeks 5–8 · U.S. geography and regions:** place the learner's state within U.S. regions; add state landforms, climate/resources, settlement patterns, landmarks, land use, and environmental change.
- **Weeks 9–12 · Indigenous nations and local/state history:** name specific Indigenous nation(s)/homelands connected to the state/region where historically appropriate; include current nation/community evidence and local/state source work.
- **Weeks 20–22 · Government/elections/civic memory:** add state constitution/government, local institutions, election-process examples, state symbols/landmarks/holidays, or public-memory cases while retaining national constitutional civics.
- **Weeks 27–30 · Historical impact/contributions:** include state/local African American, Indigenous, immigrant, worker, innovator, public-service, or institutional examples.
- **Week 33 · Global citizenship/informed action:** connect one issue to both state/local and global scales.
- **Week 35 · Research/civic capstone:** a state/local research question or safe nonpartisan civic issue is encouraged when evidence is available.

## Comparative world communities are not replaced

Japan, Ghana, Mexico, and India remain national-core comparative cases. A state may add local diaspora/community connections, trade ties, sister-city links, or migration histories, but must not replace the global-comparison competency.

## Safeguards

Do not require home addresses, immigration/citizenship status, family finances, religion, political affiliation, or private family history. Use specific Indigenous nation names and current tribal/nation sources where possible. Distinguish settlement from prior Indigenous presence and sovereignty.
