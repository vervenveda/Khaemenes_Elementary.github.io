# Grade 3 Social Studies · State & Local Localization Guide

State/local localization is a **required design feature**. Grade 3 retains its national core—U.S. geography, Indigenous histories, comparative world communities, government, economics, human rights, information literacy, and research—while requiring place-based state/local evidence where appropriate.

## Priority localization points
- **Week 4:** local history and community change.
- **Weeks 6–8:** state location within U.S. regions; climate/resources; settlement; landmarks; land use; hazards and environmental change.
- **Weeks 9–12:** specific Indigenous nations/homelands, local/state primary sources, settlement/migration/economy, and community change.
- **Weeks 19–22:** state constitution/government examples, local/state/federal/tribal jurisdictions, nonpartisan election administration, civic symbols/public memory.
- **Weeks 27–30:** state/local African American, Indigenous, immigrant, worker, innovator, public-service, and institutional contributions.
- **Weeks 33–35:** local/global connections, verification of public claims, and a state/local or broader civic research capstone.

## Comparative-world rule
Japan, Ghana, Mexico, and India remain part of the national comparative core. Educators may add a **state-to-world comparison**—for example ports/trade, migration, climate, urbanization, or public institutions—but should not replace the international cases with only local content.

## What may be localized
Maps, state/regional data, tribal-nation sources, local/state archives, public records, historical sites, current government institutions, biographies, economic sectors, environmental issues, and community questions.

## Florida example
Florida implementations may use Florida regional geography, wetlands/coasts/hazards, specific Indigenous histories and contemporary tribal nations, Florida archival/local-history sources, municipal/county/state government, state elections processes, African American and immigrant community histories, tourism/agriculture/ports, and local-global trade connections.

## Safeguards
Protect learner privacy; use public/teacher-curated evidence. Keep elections and public-issue work nonpartisan. Do not present Indigenous peoples only in the past. Distinguish state/local examples from claims meant to describe the whole United States or world.

## Non-negotiables
Localization cannot remove the four world-community cases, national constitutional/civic competencies, source-verification work, or the 80% mastery gates.
