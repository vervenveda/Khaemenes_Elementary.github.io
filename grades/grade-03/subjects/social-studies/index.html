Week 06 Civic Reasoning Studio

Inquiry: How can a community compare different needs?

Evidence task: Original scenario: A town council considers a shaded park and a new bus shelter. Identify two affected groups, evidence each needs, and a question to investigate.

Review: State one supported idea, identify one voice missing from the evidence, and revise. This activity is optional instructional evidence; the canonical Social Studies assessment is unchanged.

Week 06 · Five-day inquiry and evidence lab

Monday · Source reading: Read a fictional council agenda proposing a shaded park and a new bus shelter. Identify purpose, affected residents and the difference between a need and a preference.

Tuesday · Guided comparison: Study a fictional evidence table: 12 people request shaded seating; 18 report waiting outdoors for buses. Explain why these figures do not alone determine the best project.

Wednesday · Evidence investigation: Design three inquiry questions about cost, accessibility and likely use; mark which require records, surveys or observation instead of guessing answers.

Thursday · Challenge and critique: Compare two plausible proposals using a criteria table and explain how missing cost estimates or unheard voices could change a choice.

Friday · Civic defense and revision: Write a council recommendation with a claim, two pieces of explicitly fictional evidence, one opposing concern, and a plan to seek missing information.

Evidence standard: Identify which sources are fictional, distinguish observations from assumptions, represent more than one perspective, correct one unsupported claim, and preserve an optional written, drawn or oral explanation. Official weekly mastery, assessments, student records and teacher-review boundaries are unchanged.

Week 07 Civic Reasoning Studio

Inquiry: How do we weigh evidence and explain fair, responsible community decisions?

Monday · Read and question: A fictional town considers a safe sidewalk versus a playground repair; identify affected residents and decision makers.

Tuesday · Compare evidence: List evidence needed for both proposals such as observed hazards and estimated costs; do not invent verified figures.

Wednesday · Research and model: Build a comparison chart with access, safety and cost as criteria, leaving unknown values marked unknown.

Thursday · Challenge and revise: Explain how a different perspective could change the preferred proposal and revise a premature conclusion.

Friday · Defend with evidence: Write a civic recommendation with a claim, reason, counterargument, and research question.

Review: Identify fictional sources, explain missing perspectives and revise one inference. These instructional tasks do not change official grades, saved results, or the 80% mastery requirement.
