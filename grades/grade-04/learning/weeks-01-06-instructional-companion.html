Week 01 · Scholar launch: evidence and accuracy

Reading and evidence

A student claims that longer shadows mean the Sun is moving farther from Earth. Distinguish observation (shadow length) from explanation (a claim about distance). Write a paragraph using an observable detail and a question that could test the claim.

Worked mathematics

Build 4,305 using place-value groups: 4 thousands, 3 hundreds, 0 tens, 5 ones. Explain why 4,305 and 4,035 differ by 270. Verify by expanded form.

Scientific investigation

Mark the same object's shadow at three safe times; hold the object's location constant, note clouds and avoid looking at the Sun. Label observations separately from explanations.

Guided support: Model one example, then let the learner explain using diagrams, manipulatives, speech or writing. Challenge: Provide a counterexample or a second method and explain why it works.

Week 02 · Text evidence and place value

Reading and evidence

Compare two accounts of the same event. Label direct observations, conclusions, and missing information; quote two short details from each before explaining which claims are supported.

Worked mathematics

Compare 38,407 and 38,470 by examining each place from left to right. Explain why the tens digit determines the order, then locate both on a number line.

Scientific investigation

Create a fair comparison using identical measuring tools and document one potential confound.

Guided support: Model one example, then let the learner explain using diagrams, manipulatives, speech or writing. Challenge: Provide a counterexample or a second method and explain why it works.

Week 03 · Character and multi-digit operations

Reading and evidence

Explain how a character's decision changes the story's outcome. Defend an inference with two events rather than simply restating the plot.

Worked mathematics

Solve 4,708 + 2,596 = 7,304 by place value; verify with 7,304 − 2,596 = 4,708. Explain regrouping in the ones and hundreds places.

Scientific investigation

Plan a repeatable measurement with two trials; report differences and distinguish measurement error from real change.

Guided support: Model one example, then let the learner explain using diagrams, manipulatives, speech or writing. Challenge: Provide a counterexample or a second method and explain why it works.

Week 04 · Text structure and multiplication models

Reading and evidence

Read a cause-and-effect article, draw a chain of causes, and evaluate whether the author provides evidence for each link.

Worked mathematics

Use 24 × 13 = 24 × (10 + 3) = 240 + 72 = 312. Draw an area model and explain the distributive property.

Scientific investigation

Test two identical paper bridges with one structural feature changed. Record the same load measure and identify the variable.

Guided support: Model one example, then let the learner explain using diagrams, manipulatives, speech or writing. Challenge: Provide a counterexample or a second method and explain why it works.

Week 05 · Opinion writing and multi-digit multiplication

Reading and evidence

Write an opinion on a fictional school-garden proposal with a clear claim, two reasons, one relevant objection and a respectful reply.

Worked mathematics

Calculate 36 × 27 = (30 + 6)(20 + 7) = 600 + 210 + 120 + 42 = 972. Check using a reasonable estimate, 40 × 25 ≈ 1,000.

Scientific investigation

Collect non-identifying garden-design observations, compare tradeoffs and distinguish preferences from evidence.

Guided support: Model one example, then let the learner explain using diagrams, manipulatives, speech or writing. Challenge: Provide a counterexample or a second method and explain why it works.

Week 06 · Division and procedural clarity

Reading and evidence

Write numbered instructions for a safe classroom investigation. Exchange them with another learner and revise any ambiguous step.

Worked mathematics

Solve 156 ÷ 12 = 13 by building 12 groups of 10 (120) and 12 groups of 3 (36); check 12 × 13 = 156. Explain what would happen with 158 items.

Scientific investigation

Follow the procedure exactly twice, recording whether another person can reproduce the observations and where instructions need improvement.

Guided support: Model one example, then let the learner explain using diagrams, manipulatives, speech or writing. Challenge: Provide a counterexample or a second method and explain why it works.
