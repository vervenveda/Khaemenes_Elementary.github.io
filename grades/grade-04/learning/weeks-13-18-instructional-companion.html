Week 13: Fractions as Numbers and Equivalence

Reading and argument

Read two explanations of fraction equivalence and identify which includes a complete reason.

Worked mathematical reasoning

Show why 2/3 = 4/6 by partitioning each third into two equal pieces. Cross-check 2 × 6 = 3 × 4.

Investigation and transfer

Fold identical paper rectangles into thirds and sixths; compare areas, not merely piece counts.

Guided pathway: Annotate a model, then solve a parallel example independently. Extension: Identify an invalid assumption and defend a correction in complete sentences.

Week 14: Comparing and Ordering Fractions

Reading and argument

Explain a comparison in a paragraph using a claim, visual evidence, and mathematical reasoning.

Worked mathematical reasoning

Compare 3/4 and 5/8: 3/4 = 6/8, so 6/8 > 5/8. Place both on a 0–1 number line.

Investigation and transfer

Test whether two fraction strips are valid comparisons: the wholes must be equal in size.

Guided pathway: Annotate a model, then solve a parallel example independently. Extension: Identify an invalid assumption and defend a correction in complete sentences.

Week 15: Adding and Subtracting Fractions

Reading and argument

Critique a proposed solution, citing the exact step where the denominator was handled incorrectly.

Worked mathematical reasoning

Calculate 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8, not 5/16. Explain that eighths remain the unit. Then solve 7/8 − 3/8 = 4/8 = 1/2.

Investigation and transfer

Measure two paper strips divided into eighths and show addition by aligning equal-sized units.

Guided pathway: Annotate a model, then solve a parallel example independently. Extension: Identify an invalid assumption and defend a correction in complete sentences.

Week 16: Fractions, Measurement, and Design

Write a precise design brief explaining constraints, materials, and how success will be measured.

Worked mathematical reasoning

Investigation and transfer

Draft a classroom garden model to scale; identify where estimates may introduce error.
