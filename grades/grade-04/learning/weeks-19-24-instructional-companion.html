Week 19 · Earth materials, weathering and erosion

Evidence and reading

Compare two descriptions of a changing shoreline. Separate observations of sediment movement from explanations of what caused it.

Worked mathematical reasoning

A model loses 18 g of loose sediment in three equal trials: mean loss = 18 ÷ 3 = 6 g per trial. Explain why this is an average, not proof every trial lost exactly 6 g.

Investigation or historical inquiry

Use a tray and small amounts of water to compare erosion over bare versus covered soil; use the same slope and water volume and record limits.

Support: Model an annotated example, offer a diagram and evidence organizer, and accept oral or written reasoning. Challenge: Consider an alternative explanation or source and identify what evidence could change the conclusion.

Week 20 · Fossils, time and evidence of the past

Evidence and reading

Write a claim about an ancient environment, identify what a fossil can support, and describe what remains unknown.

Worked mathematical reasoning

In a fictional 120 cm layered rock profile, a fossil at 35 cm from the base lies 85 cm below the top. Draw and label the measurement without assuming constant age per centimeter.

Investigation or historical inquiry

Arrange illustrated layers from lower to higher and explain that relative age alone does not establish an exact calendar date.

Support: Model an annotated example, offer a diagram and evidence organizer, and accept oral or written reasoning. Challenge: Consider an alternative explanation or source and identify what evidence could change the conclusion.

Week 21 · Natural hazards and engineering

Evidence and reading

Compare two hazard-mitigation proposals and evaluate evidence, cost, accessibility and possible unintended effects.

Worked mathematical reasoning

A fictional barrier needs 48 blocks per segment for 7 segments: 48 × 7 = 336 blocks. Check with 50 × 7 = 350 as an estimate.

Investigation or historical inquiry

Test two paper building designs with the same materials, base, simulated disturbance, and measurement method; make no claim that classroom models predict real safety.

Support: Model an annotated example, offer a diagram and evidence organizer, and accept oral or written reasoning. Challenge: Consider an alternative explanation or source and identify what evidence could change the conclusion.

Week 22 · Life structures and functions

Evidence and reading

Explain how an observed structure can support a function; distinguish direct evidence from an appealing story.

Worked mathematical reasoning

A diagram shows 6 leaves on each of 9 model stems: 6 × 9 = 54 leaves. Explain why a model count need not represent all real plants.

Investigation or historical inquiry

Compare leaf photographs for vein arrangements and discuss how a trait might aid an organism without assuming every species uses it identically.

Week 23 · Indigenous histories and cultural geography

Evidence and reading

Compare two carefully attributed accounts of a place, identify whose voices are represented, and avoid treating diverse Indigenous nations as one culture.

Worked mathematical reasoning

On a fictional map with scale 1 cm = 8 km, settlements 4.5 cm apart represent 36 km. State assumptions and limits of straight-line distance.

Investigation or historical inquiry

Create a geography evidence organizer separating landforms, trade routes, community accounts and what additional local sources would be needed.

Support: Model an annotated example, offer a diagram and evidence organizer, and accept oral or written reasoning. Challenge: Consider an alternative explanation or source and identify what evidence could change the conclusion.

Week 24 · Exploration, encounter and historical perspectives

Evidence and reading

Compare differing perspectives on a historical encounter; explain why the word 'discovery' can omit peoples already living in a place.

Worked mathematical reasoning

A journey of 135 km over three equal stages averages 45 km per stage. Explain why this does not show constant daily travel or safe conditions.

Investigation or historical inquiry

Build a source-analysis table recording creator, audience, purpose, date and missing perspectives; do not treat a single historical account as definitive.

Support: Model an annotated example, offer a diagram and evidence organizer, and accept oral or written reasoning. Challenge: Consider an alternative explanation or source and identify what evidence could change the conclusion.
