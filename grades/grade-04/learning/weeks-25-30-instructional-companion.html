Week 25: Colonial regions, labor and economy

Evidence-based literacy

Compare accounts of colonial economies, identifying writers and people excluded from those accounts.

Worked mathematics

145 baskets per month over 4 months equals 145 × 4 = 580. Arithmetic does not establish whether the labor system was fair.

Inquiry

Create a source matrix including Indigenous, enslaved, free and indentured peoples rather than depicting exploitation as neutral trade.

Guided support: annotate one worked model and explain it orally or in writing. Extension: identify a questionable assumption and defend a more careful explanation.

Week 27: Decimals and money

Week 29: Lines, angles and symmetry

Evidence-based literacy

Explain the difference between appearance and a geometrically justified property.

Worked mathematics

A rectangle has four 90-degree corners; its interior angles total 360 degrees. A nonsquare rectangle has two reflection lines.

Inquiry

Fold paper shapes to test reflection symmetry and explain approximate drawing limits.

Week 30: Angle measurements

Evidence-based literacy

Critique a protractor solution where the wrong numbered scale was read.

Worked mathematics

A straight angle is 180 degrees. An adjacent 68-degree angle leaves 180 − 68 = 112 degrees.

Inquiry

Draw angle pairs, measure from the zero baseline and compare with calculated values.

Guided support: annotate one worked model and explain it orally or in writing. Extension: identify a questionable assumption and defend a more careful explanation.
