Week 31 · Poetry, drama and performance

Reading and communication

Read an original four-line verse: 'Rain taps a rhythm / Across the windowpane / I count the quiet moments / Then listen once again.' Mark sensory images and explain how the sound creates mood.

Worked mathematics

A six-minute performance includes four scenes of 75 seconds each. Four times 75 = 300 seconds = five minutes, leaving 60 seconds for transitions.

Inquiry and applied learning

Perform two readings with different pacing. Have a listener describe differences using specific observations, not assumptions about emotion.

Support: Annotate one example, then invite an oral, visual or written demonstration. Extension: Challenge an assumption, test a second method, and explain what evidence would change the conclusion.

Week 32 · Economics, entrepreneurship and budgets

Reading and communication

Compare a fictional business proposal with its expense record; distinguish a persuasive claim from supported financial evidence.

Worked mathematics

A class craft project sells 18 bookmarks at $3 each: revenue $54. Materials cost $21. The difference is $33 before other possible expenses. Explain why this is not automatically net profit.

Inquiry and applied learning

Create an accessible fictional project budget, naming material costs and any uncertainties without requiring families to spend money.

Support: Annotate one example, then invite an oral, visual or written demonstration. Extension: Challenge an assumption, test a second method, and explain what evidence would change the conclusion.

Week 33 · Digital citizenship, data and spreadsheets

Reading and communication

Compare a trustworthy data statement with one missing its source. List what a reader must know before relying on a chart.

Worked mathematics

Spreadsheet rows show 12, 15, 9, 14. Total = 50, mean = 50/4 = 12.5. Explain why a mean need not match an observed entry.

Inquiry and applied learning

Use only invented, nonpersonal data in a local table. Label columns, units, and limitations; never publish names or private student details.

Support: Annotate one example, then invite an oral, visual or written demonstration. Extension: Challenge an assumption, test a second method, and explain what evidence would change the conclusion.

Week 34 · Big-question capstone research

Reading and communication

Refine 'How can we improve our community?' into a testable question such as 'Which of two fictional routes has fewer barriers in our map model?'

Worked mathematics

A model path is 6.5 cm on a 1 cm = 4 km map, so it represents 26 km. Record assumptions about scale and straight-line distance.

Inquiry and applied learning

Prepare question, planned sources, method, accessibility considerations, and a table distinguishing data from claims.

Support: Annotate one example, then invite an oral, visual or written demonstration. Extension: Challenge an assumption, test a second method, and explain what evidence would change the conclusion.

Week 35 · Capstone studio, revision and defense

Reading and communication

Write a three-part defense: claim, two documented pieces of evidence, and one acknowledged limitation. Include a respectful response to criticism.

Worked mathematics

Compare two fictional improvement plans costing $48 and $63. Difference = $15; four units of the lower-cost plan would cost $192. Check arithmetic before judging feasibility.

Inquiry and applied learning

Revise the project after feedback, recording what was changed and why. Explain how a different result would affect the original claim.

Support: Annotate one example, then invite an oral, visual or written demonstration. Extension: Challenge an assumption, test a second method, and explain what evidence would change the conclusion.

Week 36 · Final review and Grade 5 readiness

Reading and communication

Compare an early writing sample with a revised sample, identify an evidence-based improvement, and formulate a specific next reading goal.

Worked mathematics

Solve a combined review: 3/4 = 0.75, 2.35 + 1.70 = 4.05, and 128 × 6 = 768. Check each using fractions, cents, or place value.

Inquiry and applied learning

Produce a brief inquiry reflection: question, what was learned, how it was verified, what remains uncertain, and an appropriate Grade 5 follow-up.

Support: Annotate one example, then invite an oral, visual or written demonstration. Extension: Challenge an assumption, test a second method, and explain what evidence would change the conclusion.
