# Grade 4 Social Studies · National Portability Crosswalk

This course is intentionally state-localizable because Grade 4 content varies substantially among states.

## NCSS C3
The full Inquiry Arc is embedded: questions/planning, disciplinary concepts and tools, source/evidence evaluation, conclusions, critique, and informed action.
https://www.socialstudies.org/standards/c3

## Florida Grade 4
Florida emphasizes Florida history from the pre-Columbian era through the present; primary/secondary-source analysis; Native American tribes of Florida; exploration and colonization; Fort Mose; territorial/state growth; African American community leaders; Florida Constitution/state and local government; voting/service; economics and Florida's national/global role; financial literacy including income, taxes, budgets, saving, credit, interest, and asset protection; and Florida geography.
https://www.fldoe.org/file/20885/7-3.pdf

Use `STATE_LOCALIZATION_GUIDE.md` to instantiate the state-history weeks with Florida-specific cases without narrowing the national core.

## New York Grade 4
New York centers state/local history and government, geography, Indigenous peoples, European encounter, colonial and Revolutionary eras, the new nation, westward movement/industrialization, local/state government, economics, and connections from local history to U.S. history.
https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/standards-instruction/ss-framework-k-8a2.pdf

## California Grade 4
California centers physical/human geography, Indigenous California, Spanish/Mexican periods, Gold Rush/statehood, agriculture/industry, migration, civic development, and a changing state; it also uses coordinate grids, maps/charts/pictures, and evidence about communities and regions.
https://www2.cde.ca.gov/cacs/history?c0=13%2C5

## Mastery integrity
Formal Social Studies mastery is independent of whole-grade performance. Every weekly objective gate, the midterm, and the final require 80%. Constructed responses and portfolio/capstone evidence document historical reasoning that objective questions alone cannot measure.
