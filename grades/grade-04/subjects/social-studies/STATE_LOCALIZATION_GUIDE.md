# Grade 4 Social Studies · State & Local Localization Guide

Grade 4 Social Studies varies heavily by state. This Khaemenes course keeps one national disciplinary spine while requiring educators to localize state-specific evidence in selected weeks.

## Weeks that must be localized
- Week 4: local/state research lab
- Weeks 6–8: state regions, resources, hazards, transportation, cities/rural communities
- Weeks 9–12: specific Indigenous nations/homelands and colonization/contact cases relevant to the state/region
- Weeks 23–30: territorial/statehood history, migration/removal, infrastructure/industry, Civil War/Reconstruction connections, migration and twentieth-century state change
- Week 35: state/local research capstone

## Florida implementation
Recommended Florida-specific evidence can include:
- Calusa, Tequesta, Apalachee, Timucua and other pre-colonial peoples with careful use of archaeological/historical evidence;
- contemporary Seminole Tribe of Florida and Miccosukee Tribe of Indians of Florida, plus Seminole formation and sovereignty;
- Spanish/French/British imperial periods;
- St. Augustine, San Luis de Talimali, Fort Mose;
- Adams–Onís Treaty, territorial period, Seminole Wars and forced removal/resistance;
- statehood, Civil War/Reconstruction in Florida;
- railroads, agriculture, ports, tourism, aviation/space, migration, civil-rights history, environment/hurricanes, and contemporary state economy/government;
- Florida Constitution, branches of state government, local/state problem solving;
- Florida financial-literacy benchmarks.

## California implementation
Localize to California Indigenous nations, missions/presidios/pueblos/ranchos, Mexican period, Gold Rush/statehood, migration, agriculture/industry, water/environment, state government, and contemporary regional diversity.

## New York implementation
Localize to Haudenosaunee and other Indigenous histories, Dutch/British colonial periods, Revolution, Erie Canal, westward movement, industrialization, immigration, local/state government, and state economic change.

## Safeguards
Do not require home addresses, immigration/citizenship status, family finances, religion, political affiliation, or private family history. Use specific Indigenous nation names and current nation/tribal sources where available. Do not portray land as empty before settlement. Separate commemoration from historical analysis and keep civic work nonpartisan.


## Non-negotiable mastery invariant
State/local localization may enrich or substitute appropriate examples, maps, source sets, biographies, public institutions, and historical/civic cases, but it may **not** remove the national disciplinary spine or lower the Academy's **80% mastery threshold**. Weekly subject gates, the cumulative midterm, and the cumulative final remain governed by the same mastery policy in every state.
