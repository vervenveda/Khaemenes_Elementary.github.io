Week 06 Civic Reasoning Studio

Inquiry: What makes a responsible historical interpretation?

Evidence task: Compare fictional town records created in different decades. Identify authors, dates, missing perspectives and what can be reasonably concluded.

Review: State one supported idea, identify one voice missing from the evidence, and revise. This activity is optional instructional evidence; the canonical Social Studies assessment is unchanged.

Week 06 · Five-day inquiry and evidence lab

Monday · Source reading: Read two fictional town records: a 1905 council note celebrating a new bridge and a 1930 resident letter describing unequal access. Identify author, date and purpose.

Tuesday · Guided comparison: Separate what each document directly says from what it cannot establish. Explain how time and audience may shape a source's emphasis.

Wednesday · Evidence investigation: Construct a timeline for 1905 and 1930 and a two-column corroboration table. Name a map or additional eyewitness account needed before judging access.

Thursday · Challenge and critique: Challenge the claim 'the bridge benefited everyone equally.' Explain why one celebratory record cannot prove universal access and how to avoid present-day assumptions.

Friday · Civic defense and revision: Draft a historical interpretation citing both fictional documents, acknowledging missing perspectives, and giving a specific revision if new evidence appears.

Evidence standard: Identify which sources are fictional, distinguish observations from assumptions, represent more than one perspective, correct one unsupported claim, and preserve an optional written, drawn or oral explanation. Official weekly mastery, assessments, student records and teacher-review boundaries are unchanged.

Week 07 Civic Reasoning Studio

Inquiry: How do we weigh evidence and explain fair, responsible community decisions?

Monday · Read and question: Study two fictional community records describing different estimates for a supply shipment; identify authors and dates.

Tuesday · Compare evidence: Separate first-hand observations from later summaries and explain how different units may mislead.

Wednesday · Research and model: Create a source comparison chart with claims, corroboration and missing witnesses.

Thursday · Challenge and revise: Challenge an unsupported historical conclusion based on a single record and identify needed evidence.

Friday · Defend with evidence: Draft a cautious historical interpretation, citing both fictional records and one limitation.

Review: Identify fictional sources, explain missing perspectives and revise one inference. These instructional tasks do not change official grades, saved results, or the 80% mastery requirement.
