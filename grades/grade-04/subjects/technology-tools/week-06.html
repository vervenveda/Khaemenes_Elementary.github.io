Week 06 · Technology & Tools

Division Foundations and Procedural Writing

How do we explain a process so another person can follow it?

Evidence Studio · Week 06

Study: A chart organizes observed quantities, while a graph makes comparisons visible. Labels and units prevent confusion. Use paper or an offline drawing tool; no account or internet connection is required.

Model: A class sorts 84 counters into 7 equally sized sets, making 12 counters per set. Record a table with columns “Set number” and “Counters,” listing sets 1–7 with 12 in each row. Draw a bar graph titled “Counters by set,” label the horizontal axis “Set” and the vertical axis “Counters,” and give each bar a height of 12. The identical bar heights visually verify the equal groups: 7 × 12 = 84.

Independent practice: Change the plan to 6 equal sets of 84 counters. Calculate 84 ÷ 6 = 14, revise the table and graph, and explain why each new bar is taller although the total stays 84. Include the title, units and axis labels.

Reflect: Identify one misleading graph choice (such as omitting units or using unequal intervals) and correct it. Keep the before-and-after graph as optional evidence. Existing assessments and saved grades remain unchanged.
