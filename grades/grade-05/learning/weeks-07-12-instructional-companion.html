Week 07 · Fractions with unlike denominators

Close reading and reasoning

Compare two arguments about fair sharing. Underline each premise, then explain which conclusion follows.

Worked mathematics

Compute 2/3 + 3/4 = 8/12 + 9/12 = 17/12 = 1 5/12. Verify the result is greater than one whole.

Investigation and transfer

Fold equal-sized paper rectangles into thirds and fourths, then compare on a common-twelfths grid. Explain why unlike wholes cannot be compared directly.

Support: Demonstrate a model, then ask the learner to explain using drawings, speech or writing. Challenge: Find a counterexample to a false generalization and justify an alternative approach.

Week 08 · Multiplying fractions and scaling

Close reading and reasoning

Analyze an explanation of a recipe scale change; distinguish a mathematical statement from a preference.

Worked mathematics

Calculate 3/4 × 2/3 = 6/12 = 1/2. Draw a 3-by-4 partition to justify the product rather than memorizing a rule.

Investigation and transfer

Compare scaling a model down by 1/2 with scaling it up by 2; describe how length and area change differently.

Support: Demonstrate a model, then ask the learner to explain using drawings, speech or writing. Challenge: Find a counterexample to a false generalization and justify an alternative approach.

Week 09 · Dividing fractions foundations

Close reading and reasoning

Write an explanation of how many parts of a given size fit into a whole, with units stated explicitly.

Worked mathematics

Compute 3 ÷ 1/2 = 6 because six half-units fit in three wholes. Contrast with 3 ÷ 2 = 1 1/2.

Investigation and transfer

Use paper strips to count half-units and quarter-units; explain why dividing by a number below one can increase the quotient.

Support: Demonstrate a model, then ask the learner to explain using drawings, speech or writing. Challenge: Find a counterexample to a false generalization and justify an alternative approach.

Week 10 · Volume and three-dimensional design

Close reading and reasoning

Critique a proposed shipping-box design using factual constraints rather than only descriptive adjectives.

Worked mathematics

A rectangular prism 5 cm by 3 cm by 4 cm has volume 5 × 3 × 4 = 60 cubic centimeters. Explain why the unit is cubic.

Investigation and transfer

Construct a paper net, predict whether it folds into a prism, and compare measured edges with the plan without using sharp cutting tools.

Support: Demonstrate a model, then ask the learner to explain using drawings, speech or writing. Challenge: Find a counterexample to a false generalization and justify an alternative approach.

Week 11 · Coordinate planes and data

Close reading and reasoning

Analyze a graph description for omitted units, mislabeled axes, or unsupported conclusions.

Worked mathematics

Plot (2,3) and (5,3) in the first quadrant. The horizontal separation is 5−2=3 grid units. Explain why switching x and y changes the location.

Investigation and transfer

Record fictional data pairs, graph them, and distinguish a plotted association from evidence of cause.

Support: Demonstrate a model, then ask the learner to explain using drawings, speech or writing. Challenge: Find a counterexample to a false generalization and justify an alternative approach.

Week 12 · Geometry hierarchies and classification

Close reading and reasoning

Write a precise definition of a square, explain how it is also a rectangle, and address a misleading counterclaim.

Worked mathematics

Every square has four right angles and four equal sides. A rectangle requires four right angles, so all squares are rectangles, but not all rectangles are squares.

Investigation and transfer

Sort diagram cards by properties and justify each classification; compare examples, nonexamples, and shapes drawn in unusual orientations.

Support: Demonstrate a model, then ask the learner to explain using drawings, speech or writing. Challenge: Find a counterexample to a false generalization and justify an alternative approach.
