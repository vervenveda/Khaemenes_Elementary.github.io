Week 13 · Matter: properties and measurement

Scientific reading and evidence

Contrast an observation such as 'the sample has mass 24 g' with an inference about its composition. Identify variables and units.

Worked mathematical reasoning

Three sample masses are 24 g, 27 g, and 21 g. Mean = (24+27+21)/3 = 24 g. Explain why the individual values still matter.

Investigation and reasoning

Measure safe household solids using an appropriate scale or use provided sample data. Record the measurement precision and repeat trials.

Support: Model one record with a labeled diagram and a claim–evidence–reasoning frame. Challenge: Interpret an alternative result and identify a limitation in the model.

Week 14 · Conservation of matter and mixtures

Scientific reading and evidence

Compare two explanations of dissolving and identify which makes a testable prediction.

Worked mathematical reasoning

A sealed container holds 150 g before mixing and 150 g afterward. Difference = 0 g within measurement precision; discuss why open containers may lose material.

Investigation and reasoning

Use a teacher-provided sealed-mixture data table to test mass conservation without hazardous substances; identify possible measurement errors.

Support: Model one record with a labeled diagram and a claim–evidence–reasoning frame. Challenge: Interpret an alternative result and identify a limitation in the model.

Week 15 · Forces, motion and data arguments

Scientific reading and evidence

Write claim–evidence–reasoning paragraphs comparing two motion trials; avoid declaring causation from one correlation.

Worked mathematical reasoning

A toy travels 180 cm in 6 s, averaging 30 cm/s. In another trial it travels 150 cm in 6 s, averaging 25 cm/s. Difference = 5 cm/s.

Investigation and reasoning

Build a ramp investigation plan with a consistent release position, surface and measurement system; report repeated trials and uncertainty.

Support: Model one record with a labeled diagram and a claim–evidence–reasoning frame. Challenge: Interpret an alternative result and identify a limitation in the model.

Week 16 · Earth systems: spheres and water

Scientific reading and evidence

Compare two descriptions of runoff and distinguish processes in the atmosphere, hydrosphere, geosphere and biosphere.

Worked mathematical reasoning

A fictional rain collector fills 4.5 L in three equal collection periods. Average per period = 4.5 ÷ 3 = 1.5 L; do not infer uniform rainfall.

Investigation and reasoning

Model infiltration with small amounts of water poured through sand versus soil, with equal starting volumes and an easy-clean tray.

Support: Model one record with a labeled diagram and a claim–evidence–reasoning frame. Challenge: Interpret an alternative result and identify a limitation in the model.

Week 18 · Ecosystems and energy flow

Scientific reading and evidence

Explain why a food web is more informative than a single food chain and identify the source of each claim.

Worked mathematical reasoning

A model energy chart lists 800 units at producers and 80 at one consumer level: 80/800 = 0.10 = 10%. This is model data, not a universal efficiency law.

Investigation and reasoning

Draw a local or fictional food web, labeling producers, consumers and decomposers; explain that arrows represent energy transfer and do not show all ecological relationships.

Support: Model one record with a labeled diagram and a claim–evidence–reasoning frame. Challenge: Interpret an alternative result and identify a limitation in the model.
