Week 19 · Human impact and stewardship

Close reading and evidence

Contrast evidence, interpretation and values in two fictional environmental proposals; avoid assuming a single observation proves global trends.

Worked mathematics

A cleanup group collects 18.5 kg, 21.25 kg and 16.75 kg: total 56.5 kg. Verify by combining 21.25+16.75=38 then adding 18.5.

Investigation and interpretation

Use invented survey data to compare two conservation strategies and record both advantages and unintended consequences.

Support: Use a source organizer and annotated mathematical model. Stretch: Compare alternatives, identify limitations, and defend a conclusion with evidence.

Week 20 · Stars, Sun and scale

Close reading and evidence

Distinguish what a diagram depicts from measured astronomical distance; explain why a textbook illustration may not be to scale.

Worked mathematics

A model uses 1 cm for 100,000 km. A 400,000 km distance is 4 cm. Explain why vastly different actual scales may be impractical on paper.

Investigation and interpretation

Compare scaled versus deliberately unscaled celestial diagrams and label assumptions; never view the Sun directly.

Support: Use a source organizer and annotated mathematical model. Stretch: Compare alternatives, identify limitations, and defend a conclusion with evidence.

Week 21 · Sun, Earth, Moon and patterns

Close reading and evidence

Explain observations of day/night using Earth's rotation, distinguishing it from seasonal changes.

Worked mathematics

A day is 24 hours. Four equal 6-hour intervals cover 24 hours; calculate 15 days as 15×24=360 hours.

Investigation and interpretation

Use a lamp and globe model safely with adult supervision, identify which aspects it models and which it does not.

Support: Use a source organizer and annotated mathematical model. Stretch: Compare alternatives, identify limitations, and defend a conclusion with evidence.

Week 22 · Indigenous civilizations and geography

Close reading and evidence

Compare region-specific accounts, name relevant nations or civilizations where reliable sources are supplied, and reject the idea of a single universal Indigenous history.

Worked mathematics

At a fictional map scale of 1 cm:20 km, 7.5 cm represents 150 km. Explain how terrain affects travel beyond straight-line map distance.

Investigation and interpretation

Make a source table identifying creator, date, perspective and information missing from an account; seek Indigenous-authored sources.

Support: Use a source organizer and annotated mathematical model. Stretch: Compare alternatives, identify limitations, and defend a conclusion with evidence.

Week 23 · Exploration and encounters

Close reading and evidence

Evaluate the term 'discovery' critically and compare Indigenous and European viewpoints without erasing prior inhabitants.

Worked mathematics

A fictional journey covers 840 km in 7 equal segments: 120 km each; actual travel may vary with weather and terrain.

Investigation and interpretation

Prepare a chronology with multiple accounts and label disputed claims and missing testimony.

Support: Use a source organizer and annotated mathematical model. Stretch: Compare alternatives, identify limitations, and defend a conclusion with evidence.

Week 24 · Colonization, regions and economies

Close reading and evidence

Examine how colonization shaped land use and labor; include Indigenous resistance and the experiences of enslaved people.

Worked mathematics

A hypothetical settlement records 125 units each month for 8 months: 1,000 units. Explain why quantities alone reveal nothing about coercion or justice.

Investigation and interpretation

Use a cause-and-consequence evidence matrix to distinguish economic output, human impacts, and competing source perspectives.

Support: Use a source organizer and annotated mathematical model. Stretch: Compare alternatives, identify limitations, and defend a conclusion with evidence.
