Week 06 · Arts & Music

Long Division and Remainder Decisions

How do we decide what a remainder means in context?

Evidence Studio · Week 06

Focus: Motif, dynamics and interpretation.

Study: Differentiate a repeating motif from changes in dynamics; note that volume is not the same as tempo.

Worked model: Create four measures of four beats; repeat a two-beat motif and label soft and loud passages without changing pulse.

Independent practice: Write a readable text score using claps, taps and rests; identify the repeated motif.

Challenge and revision: Evaluate whether another student could perform the score without hearing the original; improve unclear notation.

Evidence and reflection: Present a four-measure composition and artist statement defending one dynamic and one rhythmic decision. This is optional instructional evidence; formal assessments, scores and mastery remain separate.

Monday: Direct Instruction and Academic Vocabulary

Focus: artist statements

Objective: Students strengthen arts & music through artist statements while investigating 'Long Division and Remainder Decisions'.

Activity: Introduce the subject focus, connect the essential question, model advanced thinking, and define academic vocabulary.

Workshop: Arts & Music A++ workshop: create, solve, read, test, discuss, calculate, revise, or present evidence about long division and remainder decisions.

Teacher script: Name the subject: Arts & Music. Connect to the essential question: How do we decide what a remainder means in context? Model the focus skill: artist statements. Require evidence, calculation, model, vocabulary, citation note, or clear explanation. Close with a defense or reflection: The strongest evidence in my work is ____.

Evidence: Student work sample, teacher observation, oral explanation, correction/revision, and weekly subject mastery evidence.

Depth Lab · Day 1: Identify the motif, pulse and dynamics in a four-beat written musical pattern.

Reasoning check: Explain the evidence, identify an error to avoid, and revise your answer. Instructional practice is separate from graded mastery.
