Week 06 · Integrated Projects

Long Division and Remainder Decisions

How do we decide what a remainder means in context?

Evidence Studio · Week 06

Study: In a real project, a remainder is not always discarded. Its meaning depends on what is being counted and whether a partial group is useful. A clear written proposal includes the calculation, decision, evidence and revision.

Model: A community garden needs to move 53 seedlings. Each tray safely holds 8 seedlings. Long division gives 53 ÷ 8 = 6 remainder 5. Six trays hold 48 seedlings, but five remain; therefore the transport plan needs 7 trays. Check: 6 × 8 + 5 = 53. Contrast with sharing 53 paper stickers among 8 groups, where some stickers can be kept as extras rather than requiring a seventh group.

Independent practice: Draft a one-page garden delivery proposal with a heading, a labeled calculation or diagram, a seven-tray materials plan, a sentence justifying why you round up, and a short checklist for safe, careful transport. Revise the explanation after asking whether another reader could reproduce the calculation.

Reflect: Defend the decision with multiplication verification and identify a context in which a remainder should instead be kept or divided. Retain draft and revision as optional portfolio evidence; grading engines and teacher-review boundaries remain unchanged.
