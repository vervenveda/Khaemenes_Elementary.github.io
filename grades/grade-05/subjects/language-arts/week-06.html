Week 06 · Language Arts

Long Division and Remainder Decisions

How do we decide what a remainder means in context?

Evidence Studio · Week 06

Study: Two fictional reports disagree about whether a bridge repair worked. Claims require criteria, dates and sources.

Independent practice: Write a balanced comparison separating evidence from opinion.

Reflect: Identify an assumption, correct one error, and retain optional evidence. Formal assessment and mastery stay unchanged.

Monday: Direct Instruction and Academic Vocabulary

Focus: academic vocabulary

Objective: Students strengthen language arts through academic vocabulary while investigating 'Long Division and Remainder Decisions'.

Activity: Introduce the subject focus, connect the essential question, model advanced thinking, and define academic vocabulary.

Workshop: Language Arts A++ workshop: create, solve, read, test, discuss, calculate, revise, or present evidence about long division and remainder decisions.

Teacher script: Name the subject: Language Arts. Connect to the essential question: How do we decide what a remainder means in context? Model the focus skill: academic vocabulary. Require evidence, calculation, model, vocabulary, citation note, or clear explanation. Close with a defense or reflection: The strongest evidence in my work is ____.

Evidence: Student work sample, teacher observation, oral explanation, correction/revision, and weekly subject mastery evidence.

Academic Depth Lab · Day 1: Read two fictional claims about a bridge repair and distinguish the words of each claim from supporting evidence.

Explain and revise: Support your answer with a source, observation or clear argument, identify a possible misconception and correct it. Formal assessments remain separate.
