# Grade 5 Social Studies · Teacher Source Guide

Prefer free, authoritative, contextualized sources.

## Strong source families
- official tribal nation sites and cultural departments;
- Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian;
- Library of Congress;
- National Archives;
- state archives, libraries, museums, historical societies, and official public records;
- U.S. Census Bureau, USGS, National Park Service;
- Congress.gov, U.S. Courts, state constitutions, legislatures, courts, and election offices;
- Founders Online / National Archives for curated founding-era documents;
- Federal Reserve education resources for age-appropriate economics;
- Library and Archives Canada / official Canadian government sources for teacher-curated Canada comparison;
- official national archives/statistical/cultural institutions for Mexico and Latin American country cases.

## Grade 5 source routine
For important sources record creator, title/description, date, source type, purpose/audience, historical context, evidence, perspective, limitations, corroborating source, and citation/location.

Students should increasingly distinguish:
- source evidence from inference;
- claim from counterevidence;
- reporting from commentary;
- political maps/claims from actual control;
- commemoration from historical analysis;
- voluntary exchange from coerced labor or forced migration.

Do not copy full copyrighted texts or images unless public domain/licensed. Prefer links, brief excerpts, and teacher-created summaries.
