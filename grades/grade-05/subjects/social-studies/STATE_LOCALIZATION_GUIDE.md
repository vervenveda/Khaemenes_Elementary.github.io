# Grade 5 Social Studies · State & Local Localization Guide

## Academy rule

Grade 5 is nationally portable and **state-localizable by requirement**. The national course is canonical. Localize selected evidence, maps, Indigenous nations, state history, government, infrastructure, public institutions, and research topics without weakening national competencies or the 80% mastery gates.

## Required localization checkpoints

- **Week 5 · Indigenous/early-history lab:** identify specific nation(s), homelands, and current tribal/nation communities connected to the state/region when historically appropriate.
- **Weeks 9–12 · Colonial America:** add state/regional colonial, borderland, forced-labor, trade, religious, or daily-life evidence where historically relevant.
- **Week 16 · Revolutionary experiences:** include a state/local Revolutionary, Loyalist, Black American, Indigenous, women's, civilian, military, or public-memory connection where evidence exists.
- **Week 22 · Citizenship/state-local government:** use the learner's state constitution/government and local institutions alongside federal civics.
- **Week 24 · Louisiana Purchase/exploration:** localize when the state/region connects to the Purchase, western expeditions, territorial change, Indigenous diplomacy, or later mapping.
- **Week 26 · Transportation/market revolution:** use a state/local canal, road, river, port, railroad, market, industry, or communications example.
- **Weeks 27–28 · Expansion/sectionalism:** localize treaties, Indigenous removal/displacement, settlement, territorial/statehood history, slavery/free-state politics, or migration when appropriate.
- **Week 35 · Capstone:** a state/local historical inquiry is strongly encouraged when sufficient public evidence exists.

## Florida implementation

Useful Florida connections may include specific Indigenous peoples and present-day Seminole/Miccosukee communities; Spanish, French, British, and U.S. imperial/territorial periods; St. Augustine, Fort Mose, maritime/Atlantic trade; Revolutionary-era Florida under British rule; Adams–Onís and territorial transition; Seminole Wars/removal/resistance; statehood; ports, transport, migration, tourism, agriculture, and state/local government. Florida examples should plug into the national course rather than replacing it.

## California implementation

Useful connections include California Indigenous nations, Spanish/Mexican colonization, missions/presidios/pueblos, territorial transfer, Gold Rush/statehood as a bridge beyond 1850, migration, water/transportation, and state government.

## New York implementation

Useful connections include Haudenosaunee history and sovereignty, Dutch/British colonization, Revolutionary New York, Erie Canal, market growth, immigration, state/local government, and Canada-border connections.

## Safeguards

Do not require home addresses, immigration/citizenship status, religion, political affiliation, family finances, or private family history. Do not present land as empty before settler arrival. Use specific Indigenous nation names and current nation/tribal sources where possible. Keep civic activities nonpartisan.
