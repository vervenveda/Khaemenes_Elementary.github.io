# Grade 5 Social Studies · Florida State Pack
## Required overlay for Florida learners

This pack plugs current Florida-specific Grade 5 requirements into the canonical Khaemenes national course. It **adds** Florida benchmarks; it does not replace the 36-week national spine or change the Academy's 80% mastery policy.

Reference: Florida's State Academic Standards — Social Studies (2023), Grade 5.

## Required insertion map

### Weeks 5 and 8 · Florida Indigenous history and sovereignty
Use historically accurate, nation-specific evidence connected to Florida. Include present-day continuity and sovereignty. Appropriate sources may include official Seminole Tribe of Florida and Miccosukee Tribe of Indians of Florida resources, plus carefully contextualized archaeological/historical evidence for earlier peoples.

### Weeks 9–12 · Florida in the colonial Atlantic world
Where relevant, connect Spanish, French, British, African, Indigenous, and later U.S. histories to Florida. Fort Mose is especially useful for examining slavery, freedom, imperial rivalry, Black history, and Atlantic connections.

### Weeks 14–16 · Revolution and political participation
Retain the national Declaration/Revolution work and add Florida's British-period/Revolutionary context where useful. Explicitly compare Patriot, Loyalist, and neutral political choices and connect colonial participation — jury service, militia service, and elections where applicable — with lawful civic participation today.

### Weeks 19–21 · Constitution, representative government, courts, and amendment process
Florida learners must explicitly:
- explain why compromises occurred at the Constitutional Convention and identify the Great Compromise, Three-Fifths Compromise, and Electoral College;
- explain representative government;
- identify legislative, executive, and judicial branch functions associated with Articles I, II, and III;
- explain why the U.S. Supreme Court is the highest federal court;
- recognize that both the United States and Florida have supreme courts within different jurisdictions;
- explain why the Constitution contains a formal amendment process.

### Week 22 · Florida and federal government
Use the current Florida Constitution and U.S. Constitution to compare basic structural features such as preambles, articles, amendments, government frameworks, limits, and rights.

Explain federalism with examples of national powers, state powers, and U.S.–Florida cooperation.

**Current-representative task:** identify Florida's two current U.S. senators and the learner's current U.S. representative using official, current Senate/House sources. Do not hard-code officeholder names into the permanent curriculum because they change. Students should also identify constitutional qualifications, term lengths, major duties/authority, and how representation works.

### Week 34 · Voting-rights expansion and national symbols/documents
The national Week 34 now explicitly includes the Fifteenth, Nineteenth, Twenty-Fourth, and Twenty-Sixth Amendments and the Voting Rights Act of 1965.

Florida learners must additionally recognize/explain:
- the Great Seal of the United States;
- the Star-Spangled Banner as a national symbol;
- the U.S. Constitution, especially the Bill of Rights;
- the Emancipation Proclamation as a major U.S. document.

### Week 34 Florida extension · Holocaust and antisemitism
Florida Grade 5 also requires an age-appropriate definition of the Holocaust as the planned and systematic state-sponsored persecution and murder of European Jews by Nazi Germany and its collaborators from 1933–1945, plus a definition of antisemitism as prejudice or hatred directed at Jewish people and recognition of the Holocaust as an extreme historical example of antisemitism.

Teach this with factual, age-appropriate, non-graphic materials. The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum is a preferred reference. Do not use simulations, victim/perpetrator role-play, or activities that trivialize persecution.

## Florida evidence record

For each Florida overlay, save at least one source/evidence note or portfolio artifact under the matching canonical week. State-specific work supplements the randomized national mastery gate; it does not substitute for it.

## Dynamic-source rule

Current officeholders, district boundaries, election information, laws, and public data must be verified from current official sources at the time of instruction. Never freeze changeable political facts into the long-lived question bank.
