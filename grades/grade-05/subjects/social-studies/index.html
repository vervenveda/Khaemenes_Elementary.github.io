Week 06 Civic Reasoning Studio

Inquiry: How do historical evidence and constitutional principles guide civic debate?

Evidence task: Read a fictional petition and council response. Distinguish requests, rights claims, tradeoffs and the evidence needed before judging the outcome.

Review: State one supported idea, identify one voice missing from the evidence, and revise. This activity is optional instructional evidence; the canonical Social Studies assessment is unchanged.

Week 06 · Five-day inquiry and evidence lab

Monday · Source reading: Read a fictional petition asking the town to keep a public meeting accessible, and a council response citing limited space. Identify each speaker's claim and request.

Tuesday · Guided comparison: Distinguish a constitutional principle from a factual claim. Explain that an asserted right or policy violation needs legal context, not just a persuasive slogan.

Wednesday · Evidence investigation: Build an argument map with petition claim, council response, fictional evidence offered and at least two unanswered questions about accessibility or alternatives.

Thursday · Challenge and critique: Critique an argument that treats the loudest speaker as most representative. Propose a fair evidence-gathering process that protects viewpoint diversity.

Friday · Civic defense and revision: Write a balanced civic recommendation citing the fictional petition and response, acknowledging tradeoffs, and proposing a verifiable next step rather than inventing a legal verdict.

Evidence standard: Identify which sources are fictional, distinguish observations from assumptions, represent more than one perspective, correct one unsupported claim, and preserve an optional written, drawn or oral explanation. Official weekly mastery, assessments, student records and teacher-review boundaries are unchanged.

Week 07 Civic Reasoning Studio

Inquiry: How do we weigh evidence and explain fair, responsible community decisions?

Monday · Read and question: Read a fictional meeting notice proposing shared resources; distinguish request, legal claim and factual assertion.

Tuesday · Compare evidence: Map stakeholders and note which constitutional or local-government principle would need verification.

Wednesday · Research and model: Compare two public arguments about the proposal; identify evidence offered and what remains unproven.

Thursday · Challenge and revise: Critique the assumption that majority preference alone resolves every rights question; propose due process and verification.

Friday · Defend with evidence: Present a qualified recommendation with opposing reasoning, evidence gaps and a respectful next step.

Review: Identify fictional sources, explain missing perspectives and revise one inference. These instructional tasks do not change official grades, saved results, or the 80% mastery requirement.
